Humanoid Robots at CES: When Machines Start to Change Us

Introduction

What if the real revolution in robotics isn’t just about smarter machines, but about how we, as humans, begin to change in their presence? At CES this year, humanoid robots didn’t just get better—they started to subtly reshape the way people behave around them. This article explores the rise of “Physical AI” and how it’s already influencing our interactions, expectations, and even our gestures.

Why This Matters: Beyond the Demo Floor

Why should we care about how humans interact with robots? Because the way we treat machines today will shape the future of work, society, and even our sense of self. Imagine a world where robots are not just tools, but teammates—where a casual hand on a robot’s shoulder feels as natural as encouraging a colleague. This shift isn’t science fiction; it’s happening now, and it’s changing the rules of engagement between humans and technology.

What’s Happening: Physical AI in Motion

Physical AI is a new frame for understanding robotics—not just artificial intelligence that “thinks on a screen,” but AI that perceives, decides, and acts in the real world. At CES, this was on full display:

  • Whole-body control: Humanoids performed martial-arts moves, kicks, jumps, and even fought each other, requiring real-time perception and reaction.
  • Leaving the booth: Robots moved freely among attendees, not just in isolated demo areas. Many were intentionally smaller than adults, making them less intimidating and more approachable.
  • Changing human behavior: People began to treat robots less like objects and more like individuals. One attendee casually rested his hands on a robot’s shoulders—an unplanned, human gesture.
  • Performance focus: Today’s humanoids are fast and strong, though not yet as agile or graceful as humans.
  • Diversity of forms: From torso-only robots for assembly to full-body humanoids capable of human-like tasks.
  • Embodied learning: Operators used gloves to map human hand movements to robot hands, generating real-world data to train AI models.

Use Case: How Humanoids Are Shaping Human Behavior

At CES, the most striking change wasn’t a single technical breakthrough, but a gradual shift in human behavior. Humanoids are still machines, but they already trigger gestures and reactions we usually reserve for people. This is especially visible in Asia, where development is most concentrated. The question now is: will other regions catch up, and how will these new relationships with robots evolve?
